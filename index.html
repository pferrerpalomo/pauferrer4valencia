Why do I want to preside?

Closing the circle.

Everyone has a path in life, whether you believe it is already written or shaped along the way. Since the first time I chaired, back in 2023, I knew that presiding over a session in Valencia would be my closing within the organisation that has given me so much throughout all of these years.

I still remember my President back at that session, Lars Van Bourgognie, for whom Valencia was also his last EYP session. What stayed with me was the way he approached that experience: using everything he had learnt throughout his journey to support the team and make the most out of one final session. Seeing someone close their EYP path that way made me realise that, whenever my time came, I wanted to do something similar. Now it’s the moment.

I want to use this experience to transmit and put into practice all of the knowledge and crazy ideas that I have gathered in all my past sessions one last time, hopefully to give something back to the organisation that helped me turn into the person I am today.

But… why closing my path as a President? The answer is simple, academic teams have always felt like a safe space for me in EYP. They have been the team where I have grown, experienced and enjoyed EYP the most. Thus, presiding one last time would give me the opportunity to create one last team built around the core values that made me enjoy every session I have attended: diversity, creativity, friendship and personal growth.

And most importantly… why Valencia? Well, because it is where everything started for me. Ever since that first session as a delegate in 2021, I have participated in every session of Valencia. This regional session has seen me grow, try out new roles, and discover my passion for EYP. For this reason, to close the circle, I would love to end my journey in Valencia, seeing how the new generation begins falling in love with this organisation in the same way I did six years ago.

Question 01

What do you plan for VPs, Chairs, and delegates to get from participating in this session?

For me, participating in an EYP session should mean much more than simply fulfilling a role or producing a good academic output (which of course should also be a pillar). What truly made me stay in this organisation throughout the last six years has been the opportunity to meet people from completely different backgrounds, build meaningful bonds and continuously discover new things about myself and others.

In this line, regardless of whether someone joins Valencia RSC as a VP, Chair or Delegate, I want them to leave the session having enjoyed the process, grown from the experience and, most importantly, with something they will still remember years later. In the end, as time passes, we do not remember how good our resolution was, whether we submitted something exactly on time or how perfectly everything went according to plan. What remains are the people we met, the moments we shared and the memories we gathered along the way.

For this, when it comes to the Academic Team, I envision a team that operates under three main pillars:

Creativity & Ownership
I want people to feel comfortable thinking differently, taking initiative and bringing their own ideas to life. Everyone should have the space to experiment, take responsibility and actively contribute to shaping the session.
Personal Growth
I want Valencia to be an opportunity to learn from each other, explore our strengths and weaknesses and progressively become more confident in our roles.
FUN
Yes, in capital letters. EYP is also about building meaningful human connections, and I want us to genuinely enjoy the journey together.

For my VPs, I want Valencia RSC 2027 to be a space where they can truly explore the role and add their own “touch” to the session. After all, I see VPs as an extension of my position as president. This doesn’t mean that they should be exactly like me, but rather that we should all work together towards the same direction while each bringing our ideas and ways of doing things.

For my chairpersons, I want to build a diverse team where people with different levels of experience, profiles and visions can work together and complement each other. Beyond the academic side of the session, my goal is to pay special attention to the interpersonal dynamics of the team, so that Chairs can leave Valencia not only proud of the work they have done, but also feeling that they truly belonged to a team.

Finally, for the delegates, I want Valencia to be a first glimpse into what EYP can truly offer. My impact on them will not be as direct as with the Academic Team, but I aim to ensure that they understand that EYP is way more than just debating and building a resolution, but an international network where they can build their own path. After all, this might be my last session in “La Familia”, but it will be the first EYP memory for many of them. Hopefully, Valencia RSC 2027 can show them the same reasons why I fell in love with EYP back in 2021.

Question 02

What academic and personal aspects do you plan on focusing on?

In my opinion, the academic and personal sides are equally important within an academic team, as it’s the combination of both what make up the so called “perfect” chair (if that is even a concept). Due to this, I believe they should complement and reinforce each other throughout the entire process.

Bearing this in mind, I want to structure the pre-session process around a project called “Viatje a la Terreta”, where the whole path till the session is unified and communicated to chairpersons as single journey. With this, we would ensure that teambuilding, TO writing and modules are integrated in a single process, making it easier to understand for chairpersons and giving them a feel of advancement.

Moving on to the academic side, my main goal is to make sure that the tools we are giving to both chairs and delegates are accurate, engaging and provide real value. I want the Academic Board to rethink each module and explore practical methods such as simulations, open debates and gamified competitions. In the end, if we want chairpersons to use the tools we give them, we need to make those tools worth using.

I would also like to work on Topic Overviews, giving chairpersons more flexibility and freedom in the TO writing process to adapt the topics to what excites them the most (bearing in mind the limits within a regional session, we dont wanna go delulu). This could mean a more flexible structure, integrating a media section and allowing chairpersons to change existing topics or even propose topics on their own.

When it comes to the personal side, my main focus will be taking special care of team dynamics and interpersonal soft skills before, during and after the event, balancing academic quality with a friendly and fun environment. Finally, I want every member of my team to feel safe, accompanied and supported throughout all of the journey, adapting each member’s experience to their needs.

Question 03

What common practice in Academic Teams would you change and how?

After talking with fellow eypers and observing the reality in sessions throughout these last years, I think there is a relative consensus regarding the decrease in the academic quality at EYP sessions.

If I had to choose one common practice I would change, I would focus on the way we structure General Assembly. In my opinion, adding to the increasingly worrying use of AI to fully generate speeches and points, we are beginning to lose genuine back-and-forth debates.

Just think about it… during Rounds of Debate, how many times does the proposing committee deliberately ignore some of the questions raised, or simply lack enough time to properly address them? Probably more times than we would like.

Therefore, for Valencia RSC 2027 I would like to introduce a reworked GA procedure that Dorian Morgenroth and I developed in my last session as a president, taking inspiration from a procedure originally created by Elia Pistis.

Open GA procedure ↗

The idea is not to fully change or reinvent the whole structure, but to make small changes that reinforce the nature of the debates happening during GA. By moving away from a “many points, one answer” structure towards a more one-to-one exchange, each intervention receives an immediate response from the proposing committee before moving to the next point.

Question 04

If you were a delegate again, which role of GA would you want to do?

If you had asked me the first time I was a delegate, I would have taken the “defense speech” straight away. As a person full of insecurities and scared of talking in front of a big crowd, all I wanted was a role where I could prepare in advance and have every word perfectly structured and rehearsed before it was my turn to speak.

However, after all of these years of growth within and out of EYP, now I would not hesitate to choose the round of debate. I would feel much more comfortable improvising and defending the ideas which I had been developing with my colleagues during the session. I am sure no one would leave me speechless there (jajaja).

In a way, this change reflects what EYP has given me throughout the years, turning my fears into strengths and making me a more open person that does not fear facing new challenges.
